## Native and access-boundary evidence

Bifrost's 36 native `unsupported` cells are catalog/selector-grounded. The
separate positive control remains `inconclusive` with `partial_discovery` and
does not promote any native cell. The <a href={evidenceUrl(snapshot, 'reports/releases/v0.7.1/bifrost-native-mapping-audit.json')}>mapping audit</a>
keeps those two facts separate.

The Bifrost access record includes 17 initial sandboxed executions and five
approved full elevated repeats. The paired controls have equal outcomes across
access modes; that establishes outcome equality for those controls, not timing
parity. Desktop activity and cache state remain uncontrolled. The retained
<a href={evidenceUrl(snapshot, 'reports/releases/v0.7.1/bifrost-access-control-results.json')}>access-control evidence</a>
states the scope explicitly.

OpenTaint's native qualification is source-selector grounded: the shipped
servlet/Spring selectors do not match the environment-variable fixture source
shape. The product probe shows activation on a servlet control, while its
functional-probe timings are diagnostic and excluded from cold, warm, and
overhead latency. See the <a href={evidenceUrl(snapshot, 'reports/releases/v0.7.1/opentaint-native-category-audit.json')}>selector audit</a>
and <a href={evidenceUrl(snapshot, 'reports/releases/v0.7.1/opentaint-functional-timing-exclusion.json')}>timing exclusion</a>.

Every invoked case also retains its phase-timing sidecar beside raw evidence;
the [latency page](./latency/) interprets those sidecars without conditioning
any correctness outcome on a timing value.
