DataFlowBench's latency-characterization tier. It stands **beside** the
correctness scorecards, on its own page, and never as a column inside one.

:::caution[This corpus was not re-measured in v0.6.1]
Every number on this page is the corpus **frozen at v0.6.0**, carried into
this snapshot unchanged. v0.6.1 is a correctness re-run of the twenty Bifrost
slices; it did not re-run the latency tier, which is measured under its own
settle-gated protocol on a quiet machine and is not a by-product of a
correctness run.

So the Bifrost rows below name **v0.10.7**, not the v0.10.8 pin this
snapshot's correctness pages report. That is deliberate. Those labels are
descriptions of retained evidence, and re-labelling them to v0.10.8 would
assert a measurement that was never performed. The consequence is worth
stating plainly: **do not read these timings as v0.10.8's**, and do not read a
correctness change on the other pages as having any timing consequence here.
The next freeze that re-measures the corpus will say so.
:::

The contract governing every number here —
[`docs/latency-tier.md`](https://github.com/BrokkAi/dataflowbench/blob/main/docs/latency-tier.md)
— was written and merged **before any timestamp was captured**, and it is
explicit about why. DataFlowBench is published by the vendor of one of the
engines measured, and a latency page assembled after the numbers were known,
with its phase boundaries and its aggregation chosen afterwards, would deserve
exactly the skepticism it would get. So the decomposition rule, the exclusion
list, the per-adapter granularity, and the aggregation were all fixed while
every number was still unmeasured. The four analyzers added in v0.6.0 took
their granularity rows by
[Amendment A12](https://github.com/BrokkAi/dataflowbench/blob/main/docs/latency-tier.md#amendments),
dated and landed **before** this page published any of their numbers, rather
than being backfilled into the original table.

The page also carries a second, separately labelled figure: the **warm marginal
cost** of one more case in a tool process that has already started, added by
[Amendment A15](https://github.com/BrokkAi/dataflowbench/blob/main/docs/latency-tier.md#amendments)
and likewise preregistered before the first warm number was measured. It exists
because the cold rows below span three runtimes, and read across runtimes they
overstate the steady-state gap — a difference the vendor publishing this
benchmark happens to win. The correction is a direct measurement, not an
estimate subtracted from anything: **no cold number on this page changes, and
the warm figure is never substituted for one.**
